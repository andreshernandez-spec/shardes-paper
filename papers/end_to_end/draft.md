## Abstract

Evolution strategies (ES) fine-tune language models without backpropagation and have
matched reinforcement learning (RL) on single tasks such as Countdown. We ask whether they
do so on a mixed post-training stream from a strong start. Starting from
Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B-DPO, we run ES on the prompts and the rollout count of the Tulu 3.1
GRPO run and score both on prompts outside their training exposure, with the RL side taken
from the released checkpoints. Three results. At 16 full-weight members and sigma 5e-4,
adapted from the reference configuration, the model ends 0.25 +- 0.09 reward points below
its start after the data of 480 RL steps, where the released step-480 checkpoint is 2.12
+- 0.11 above it. With 512 low-rank members, eight updates gain +0.14 +- 0.06 on the
pooled score, with instruction following up and MATH down, at 32 times RL's rollouts and
an estimated 15 times its GPU-seconds per unit of gain. With 1,024 members, a screen and a
step per matrix type chosen from one-update measurements, eight updates lose 0.32 +- 0.07:
instruction following rises and the reasoning tasks fall. The measurements behind these
runs fit the signal-and-diffusion account of Hoy et al.: the update's component along the
gradient of the run's loss, computed on a reconstruction of its first batch, is 12% of the
largest a zeroth-order estimate can have; its odd response per unit does not depend on the
population while its even response falls as 1/N; and the even response is three to six
times larger on MATH than on IFEval. On Countdown the same model's perturbations show a
cross-prompt response correlation of 0.30 against 0.004 on the stream. The matched-budget
shortfall is large and the task trade-off is measured; which of the estimator's
assumptions fails over many updates is not identified.

## 1. Introduction

Evolution strategies perturb a model's weights at random, score each perturbed copy, and
move the weights toward the copies that scored well. They need no backward pass, no
optimizer state and no reference model, and their copies communicate only their scores.
Qiu et al. (2025) report ES at 30 members matching or exceeding GRPO on Countdown and on
conciseness; EGGROLL (Sarkar et al., 2025) makes populations of thousands affordable with
low-rank perturbations; Hoy et al. (2026) find ES matching GRPO in single-task accuracy
with updates of far larger norm, and derive why. We are unaware of a comparison on a mixed
stream of verifiable tasks from an instruction-tuned start at matched data and rollouts,
which is where RL is used in practice.

This paper makes that comparison on Tulu 3.1 (Lambert et al., 2024): the same start, the
same prompts in the same order, the same number of generated answers, scored the same way,
with the RL side taken from the released intermediate checkpoints so that the baseline is
somebody else's tuned run. The question is whether ES learns the stream at the matched
budget and, where it does not, which measured quantities differ from RL's. Table 1 gives
the outcomes; Sections 5 to 7 measure the terms of the update.

**Table 1. Outcomes.** Change in reward (0 to 10, the run's verifiers, greedy decoding)
against the DPO start, paired over prompts, with the prompt block scored.

| run | generated answers | block | pooled change | IFEval | MATH | GSM8K |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| released RL, step 120 | 92,160 | RL steps 121 to 160 | +1.26 +- 0.11 | +2.19 | +0.47 | +0.23 |
| released RL, step 480 | 368,640 | RL steps 481 to 520 | +1.87 +- 0.11 | | | |

| released RL, step 40 | 30,720 | RL steps 121 to 160 | +0.44 +- 0.11 | +0.81 | -0.05 | +0.20 |

The scope is one model and one stream, one seed per configuration, greedy decoding with
the run's verifiers as the measure, and an inference engine holding bf16 weights.
Section 2 states it once; caveats travel with the numbers they bound.

## 2. Setup

**Model and data.** The start is `allenai/Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B-DPO`; the RL run is the
GRPO training of `allenai/Llama-3.1-Tulu-3.1-8B` (open-instruct at version 3f37c29),
which took 48 prompts and 16 samples per prompt per step from a stream of 29,689
verifiable prompts (GSM8K, MATH and IFEval-style constraints) that we rebuilt from the
released training code and data, assuming the published command. Its released
intermediate checkpoints at steps 40 to 480 are the RL side; no RL was trained here, so
its training exposure, gradients and optimizer path are known only from the recipe.

**Matching.** One ES iteration uses 16 members, each decoding the next 192 prompts of the
stream, the prompts of four RL steps, so an iteration generates the 3,072 answers those
four steps generate. Iteration g sees RL steps 4g+1 to 4g+4.

**ES.** Adapted from Qiu et al.'s configuration (30 members, sigma 1e-3, alpha 5e-4): 16
members, to match the run's 16 samples per prompt, and sigma = alpha = 5e-4. Members are
Gaussian perturbations of all 8.03 billion weights (each 44.8 from the start in Euclidean
length); a member's fitness is its mean reward over its prompts under greedy decoding; the
update is `u = (alpha / N) sum_m z_m E_m` with `z` the population-standardized fitness and
`E_m` the unit-scale perturbation. Members and updates are computed by the shardes
library's seed-regenerated noise streams and written into a vLLM engine that generates;
the engine's weights were checked against the library's bits at every restore and on one
member every ten iterations. Sections 6 and 7 use rank-1 perturbations `sigma a b^T` of
the 224 attention and MLP matrices (6.98 billion parameters) in mirrored pairs, served as
LoRA adapters built from their seeds; an adapter's effect on the log-probabilities of 32
fixed answers agrees with the same perturbation merged into the weights at correlation
0.997 and slope 1.01 for one member and 0.995 and 0.94 for another.

**Measurement.** Reward is the run's verifiers' score, 10 for a pass and 0 otherwise.
Two prompt blocks serve as evaluation: RL steps 121 to 160 (1,920 prompts, outside the
exposure of the step-40 to step-120 checkpoints and of every ES run) and RL steps 481 to
520 (1,920, outside every compared model's exposure). The step-480 checkpoint trained on
the first block. Both blocks together also set the step size and the per-type steps of
the runs in Section 7, so for those runs they are validation data, not an untouched test;
no fresh test block was reserved, and the review that led to this revision names it the
first thing to add. Comparisons are paired over prompts; standard errors are over
prompts, conditional on the recorded weights, and say nothing about variation over
seeds. Because the engine holds bf16 weights, a perturbation's length is reported as what
survived the rounding (its effective length) where that differs from the nominal.

## 3. The update, decomposed

Hoy et al. (2026, Section 6) write the ES displacement as a signal term that follows the
gradient and a diffusion term that does not, with the diffusion's expected squared
displacement `alpha^2 T d / N` after `T` updates and a gradient-aligned fraction of order
`N / d`. We use the same decomposition for one update and measure its terms. For a
member's fitness written as its linear response plus a remainder,
`f(theta + sigma E) = f(theta) + sigma g . E + r(E)`, with `g` the gradient of the
smoothed objective at the start, and `rho` the correlation between the standardized
fitness and its linear part, the update's expectation over the noise is, for isotropic
Gaussian perturbations and to first order in the response,

    E[u] = alpha rho g_hat,                                        (1)

a step of at most `alpha` along the gradient's direction, while the remainder of `u` has
characteristic length `alpha sqrt(d / N)`. Rank-1 perturbations are not Gaussian in the
parameter space and mirrored pairs change the remainder's coefficient; for them (1) is a
local approximation whose terms are measured rather than derived, with `d` the number of
perturbed parameters.

Applying an update both ways, `theta_0 +- lambda u`, separates two responses exactly:
the **odd response**, half the difference of the two rewards, and the **even response**,
their mean minus the start's. To first order the odd response is `lambda` times the
gain along `u`, and to second order the even response is `lambda^2` times the cost of
`u`'s remainder, which depends on the update's covariance and the reward's curvature and
not on its direction; both also contain higher-order terms, and the greedy decode's
discontinuities make them finite-length responses rather than derivatives. With `G` the
odd response per unit and `C` the even response per unit squared, a quadratic model nets
`lambda G + lambda^2 C`, largest at `lambda* = G / (2 |C|)` with

    fitted net* = G^2 / (4 |C|).                                    (2)

Three consequences are measured below. `G` should not depend on the population and `C`
should fall as 1/N (Section 7). The step cannot change the ratio of the two parts, only
where the quadratic balances (Section 5). And `G` compared with the slope along the
gradient of the run's own loss says how much of the gradient the ranking captured
(Section 6).

## 4. ES does not learn the stream at 16 members

Table 1's first rows: thirty iterations at sigma 5e-4 give no detectable change against
a control whose members receive random fitness (slope 1.7 standard errors from zero; the
1e-3 and 2e-3 runs of the same pilot were made before a nondeterminism in the update's
accumulation was found and fixed, and they do not exclude those settings). Continued to
120 iterations, the sigma-5e-4 run ends 0.26 +- 0.09 above the control and 0.25 +- 0.09
below its start, where the released step-480 checkpoint is 2.12 +- 0.11 above the run.
The backend reproduces the library bit for bit and learns Countdown faster than the
reference implementation at identical settings (mean evaluation reward 0.182 against
0.159 over updates 5 to 100), so the null is not an implementation fault among the checks
made.

The released run moves the weights little: the step-120 checkpoint is 0.476 from the
start and step 480 is 1.134, against 61 for ES after the data of 120 steps and 45 for a
single member's perturbation. On 768 training prompts of RL steps 1 to 16, the reward
along the displacement to step 120 rises +0.85 at effective length 0.32 and +1.02 at
0.48; along random directions of the same lengths it does not move (six changes within
1.3 standard errors of zero). The cosine between the run's moves to step 120 and to step
240 is 0.51; consecutive ES updates are independent random directions.

## 5. One update's odd response is about its even response

The first update of the 16-member run, applied as `theta_0 +- lambda u` on the 3,840
prompts of both later blocks, at lambda 4 and 10:

| per unit update, 16 members | odd response per unit | even response per unit squared |
|---|---|---|
| ranking on 768 prompts | +0.004 +- 0.003 (at most 0.010) | -0.0042 +- 0.0005 |
| expected even response from the members' mean change over N | | -0.0038 |

Random weightings of the same 16 directions give odd responses of +-0.011, so the ranked
update is not distinguishable from one. At the run's step the quadratic net is about
nothing, as the run showed. Mirrored members (each perturbation and its negative) show
that two thirds of what makes a member score well is the same whichever sign its
perturbation has, which a weighted sum of perturbations does not inherit.

## 6. The update's component along the run's gradient

We computed the gradient of the run's loss at the start on a reconstruction of its first
batch: the 192 prompts of RL steps 1 to 4, 16 answers each newly sampled by the start at
temperature 1.0, the run's verifiers and advantage formula, the token mean per micro-batch
of 2 as the run forms it. It is not the run's own batch, whose samples are not released,
and it pools four rollout steps into one gradient; the KL term's gradient is zero at the
start, and the run's two optimizer minibatches and Adam state are not modelled. 91 of the
192 groups have unequal rewards; the rest contribute nothing to this gradient, as the
prompts no member changes contribute nothing to an ES update.

The projection `u . g_hat / alpha` is `rho` times the cosine between the estimator's own
gradient and this one, and a ranking with no signal gives noise of standard deviation
`1 / sqrt(N)`:

| update | projection toward reward (noise sd) |
|---|---|
| 1,024 low-rank members | +0.120 (0.031) |
| the first 512 of the same members; the same 512 directions decoded again | +0.123, +0.116 (0.044) |
| 16 dense members | unresolvable (0.25) |

These are one population, not three; the response is 3.9 standard errors from zero over
prompts for it.

The gradient is concentrated: 993 of 7 billion elements carry 24% of its squared norm.
Along it, the reward on the two later blocks rises 7.3 +- 1.3 per unit effective length
at the smallest usable length (0.022; the alignment of the rounded displacement with the
gradient was not recorded), and the model is broken by length 0.08. Along the unit sign
vector, the direction of Adam's first step, the slope is 0.76 +- 0.06 at length 0.60.
A zeroth-order estimate aligned with this gradient would gain, to first order,
`alpha` x 7.3 = 0.0037 +- 0.0007 per unit, 0.8 +- 0.15 of the odd response the
1,024-member update showed. The two are responses of different directions on different
objectives (the run's sampled loss, ES's greedy fitness), so this compares the tested
directions and bounds no other estimator. Among the estimators tested, sampled fitness
with common random numbers, mirrored pairs, a log-probability contrast of fixed answers,
and a step per matrix type, none raised the odd response per unit above the greedy
reward's.

**Why the scores carry little on this stream.** At 16 members a member changes 9.8% of
the greedy outcomes, and the share of the variance of its fitness over 192 prompts that
is its own (its reliability) is 0.12 (90% interval 0.01 to 0.47); 8 samples per prompt at
temperature 1.0 with common random numbers give 0.11, and mirrored pairs are no better per
rollout. Answers depart from the start's within the first dozen tokens on 86 to 90% of
prompts, where the start's top two tokens are within a few bf16 steps of each other, and
continue as fresh draws; a perturbation moves an uncertain prompt's success probability
by about 8 points, and the split-half covariance of member effects across prompts, divided
by their de-noised variance, is 0.0095 (0.004 over all prompts). Scored instead by the
log-probability contrast of fixed right and wrong answers the start had sampled, exact to
the bit on a rescore, that statistic is 0.0024 and 36% of the ranking is common to
prompts, half of it a length artifact. On Countdown, with the same start and the same 16
directions, the statistic is 0.30 (0.49 on uncertain prompts) and 42 to 68% of the
ranking is common to prompts; for a 0.5B model that learns Countdown in 100 updates, 1.06
and 0.94. The contrast shows the response's dependence on the task and its scoring for a
fixed model; it does not by itself say that one shared behaviour is the cause.

## 7. More members

Members as rank-1 perturbations of every attention and MLP matrix, in mirrored pairs,
served as LoRA adapters built in memory from their seeds, 64 per batch. 1,024 members
decoded the first 192 prompts; the update from the first N of them was applied as in
Section 5 on the two later blocks.

| members | odd response per unit | even response per unit squared | fitted net* (Eq. 2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16, dense | +0.004 +- 0.003 | -0.0038 | 0.001 |
| 128 | +0.0037 +- 0.0011 | -0.00054 | 0.006 +- 0.004 |
| 512 | +0.0047 +- 0.0006 | -0.00015 | 0.036 +- 0.010 |
| 1,024 | +0.0047 +- 0.0004 | -0.000077 | 0.071 +- 0.016 |
| released RL, mean per four steps over its first 120 | | | 0.040 |

The populations are nested. The odd response per unit does not change with N and is 11
standard errors from zero over prompts at 1,024; the even response falls as 1/N and is 1.2
to 1.3 times a dense update's at the same length. Both change with the length: at 1,024
members the odd response per unit is 0.0056 at length 44.7 and 0.0047 at 111.7, the even
coefficient -1.2e-4 and -7.7e-5, so the fit is local and the errors of `G` and `C` are not
independent. The one step scored near the fitted optimum (1,024 members, length 44.7)
changed the reward by +0.057 +- 0.053. The RL figure is a mean over 120 steps, not a
first-step gain.

**Eight updates.** At 512 members and the step of Eq. 2, along the stream (Table 1): +0.018
+- 0.055 after four updates and +0.141 +- 0.061 after eight, against a predicted +0.29
if each update kept its fitted net; IFEval +0.47, MATH -0.46, GSM8K +0.10. The RL
checkpoint at step 40, on the first block, has +0.44 with IFEval +0.81 and MATH -0.05.
The ES displacement after eight updates (83 long) has cosine 1e-5 with the released
displacement to step 120: the two share a task profile, not a direction in parameter
space.

**The even response is task-specific.** At 1,024 members and length 112 it is -1.07 on
MATH, -0.48 on GSM8K and -0.19 on IFEval; at 16 dense members and length 112, -0.78,
-0.40 and -0.25. A per-type measurement of the 1,024-member update (each matrix type's
part applied alone) put 17% of the odd response and 23% of the even response in v_proj,
2% of the parameters, and 19% and 10% in up_proj; a step per matrix type from those
measurements had a fitted net 1.5 times the uniform one on the same per-type fit (2.4
against the full update's separate fit; 1.0 over the types whose even response was
resolved). Eight updates at 1,024 members with that step, a screen that decodes with the
full population only the prompts on which 128 screening members did not all score alike
(on the measured population, the screened update's coefficients have cosine 0.997 with
the unscreened ones), and an update length of 80 to 86 against 43 without the per-type
step and 31 in the 512-member run, lost 0.21 +- 0.06 after four updates and 0.32 +- 0.07
after eight: IFEval +0.65 +- 0.09, GSM8K -0.72 +- 0.14, MATH -1.81 +- 0.16. The odd
response's task profile held (IFEval rose 0.081 per update against 0.058 at 512 members),
and the reasoning tasks fell further than the additive quadratic model allows: it
predicted +0.15 per update from the same per-type record. Unequal task costs explain the
trade between tasks, not the pooled loss; at least one of the model's assumptions
(coefficients stable as the weights move, quadratic response at these lengths, additivity
across matrix types, the screen's equivalence to the full ranking) fails over eight
updates, and these records do not isolate which. The three runs differ in population,
step, screen and per-type weighting at once, so they do not isolate any one of them.

## 8. Compute, estimated

On one H200, at per-token rates measured on the start's greedy answers, per 192 prompts:
the released run's four steps decode 3,072 answers (100 GPU-s at 10,600 tokens/s), take a
gradient on them (538 GPU-s at 4,465 tokens/s for a forward and backward pass with
gradient checkpointing, micro-batches of 2 sorted by length) and a reference forward (146
GPU-s), 784 GPU-s, with no optimizer, communication or pipeline idling timed. One ES
update at 512 members decodes 98,304 answers in 4,744 GPU-s at 7,000 tokens/s with LoRA
members (a factor 1.5 below the base model's rate). Per unit of fitted one-update gain
(0.037 against the RL run's 0.040 mean per four steps) ES's estimated GPU-seconds are 6.6
times RL's; over the eight updates at 512 members, with measured iteration time and the
first block's gains for both, about 16 times. The screen spends 62% of the rollouts; a
length cap gives up at least as large a share of the outcome changes that rank the
members as of the tokens it saves; twice the adapters per batch decodes 21% slower.

## 9. Discussion

Both methods see the same prompts and generate the same answers, compared within each
prompt's group. The released run's step is its minibatch gradient, carrying sampling
noise and the surrogate's bias but no exploration component; the ES update has a
component along that gradient of 12% of the largest possible and a remainder of
characteristic length `alpha sqrt(d / N)` whose even response sets the step. On this
stream the remainder's cost equals the odd response at 16 members, and with 1,024 members
the quadratic fit is favourable for one update and fails over eight, most on the
reasoning tasks. On Countdown the same model's scores carry a shared response an order of
magnitude larger. Which property of a task sets that share, and whether a step sized by
the most fragile task's cost lets a large population accumulate what it gains, are the
questions the next stage of this work tests by prediction (`docs/end_to_end/10`).

## 10. Related work

Qiu et al. (2025) fine-tune models with ES at 30 members and sigma 1e-3 and report parity
with or gains over GRPO on Countdown (Qwen2.5-3B from 10.0% to 60.5% against 43.8% for
the strongest reported RL comparator), conciseness, Sudoku and ARC (the last with a 14B
model), and math with a math-specialized 7B model against external RL checkpoints.
EGGROLL (Sarkar et al., 2025) serves low-rank perturbations at populations of 256 to
8,000, reports Qwen3-4B on DeepScaleR at 2,048 members matching an external RL baseline's
benchmark average, and its linear-layer benchmark runs near batch-inference speed; its
pretraining result uses about 180 times the backpropagation baseline's GPU-hours. Hoy et
al. (2026) match GRPO in single-task accuracy across four tasks trained sequentially,
derive the signal-and-diffusion decomposition this paper measures, and find update norms
87 to 107 times GRPO's; Abdi et al. (2026) find the same norm gap with faster forgetting.
Malladi et al. (2023) show the zeroth-order slowdown scales with the Hessian's effective
rank rather than the parameter count; our even responses measure that coupling directly
rather than inferring it from the remainder's norm. Filtering prompts whose samples all
score alike is used in GRPO training (Yu et al., 2025); the screen of Section 7 scores a
subset of members on all prompts and the rest on the live ones, a different device.

## 11. Conclusion

On the Tulu 3.1 stream, from the same start and with the same generated answers, ES at
16 members does not learn and the released GRPO checkpoints do, by 2.12 +- 0.11 after the
data of 480 steps. The update's component along the run's gradient is 12% of the largest
a zeroth-order estimate can have, and the remainder's cost equals its gain at that
population; more members raise the fitted gain per update in proportion to N, and the
cost that remains falls on the reasoning tasks first, so the pooled gain over eight
updates was +0.14 at 512 members and -0.32 at 1,024 with a per-type step. These are the
outcomes of the configurations tested, with one seed each, and the measured terms behind
them; whether another step size or estimator does better on this stream is not
established either way.
