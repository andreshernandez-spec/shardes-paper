\begin{abstract}
Evolution strategies (ES) are often described as cheap to distribute because devices exchange only scalar fitness scores. This approach still requires every device to reconstruct the full parameter update. We compare it with splitting that computation across devices and combining partial updates with an all-reduce. Using shardes, a common JAX implementation, we study full-rank and low-rank perturbations on A100 GPUs and TPU v5e chips, with a transformer-block benchmark and Qwen2.5-0.5B. The faster placement depends on whether the computation saved outweighs the communication added. On the block benchmark with eight devices in one host, all-reduce gives 1.2–5.6× speedups for full-rank perturbations. For low-rank perturbations at the larger matrix width, it takes 4–46\% longer than replication. Qwen2.5-0.5B shows the same placement tradeoff, while a slow connection between two A100 hosts makes replication faster for large full-rank updates. At equal population, rank-1 updates align with the loss-reducing gradient direction about as well as full-rank updates at 0.5B and 1.5B parameters. On Countdown, the tested ranks reach similar final accuracy: full rank learns faster per sample early on, while rank-1 updates run 1.44× faster with matched evaluation batching. Code, configurations, and results are released.
\end{abstract}
