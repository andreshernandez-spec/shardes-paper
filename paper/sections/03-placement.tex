\section{Computing the update}
\label{sec:placement}

Each of $D$ devices holds the full model and evaluates $N/D$ candidates. An
\emph{all-gather} collects their scalar scores and makes the complete list available on
every device. Every device ranks that list to obtain the weights $w_i$. The weighted
sum can then be formed in two ways (Figure~\ref{fig:placements}).

\paragraph{Replicated update (A).} Every device reconstructs the contributions from all
$N$ candidates and forms the complete sum. Only scalar arrays are communicated. Our
implementation gathers both the raw scores and, in a second operation, the weights
computed from their ranks: two arrays of $4N$ bytes for float32 values.

\paragraph{Split update (B).} Each device reconstructs only its $N/D$ contributions.
An all-reduce adds the partial sums and returns the result to every device. It
communicates the $4N$-byte score array and a $4P$-byte update array. These are the
sizes passed to the communication operations; bytes sent over the connection also
depend on the collective algorithm.

\paragraph{The decision rule.} Splitting helps when the computation it saves takes
longer than the communication it adds. Write $C_A$ for the time to reconstruct the
whole sum and $C_B$ for the time to reconstruct one device's share. Since evaluation
and applying the update are common to both placements, the generation times satisfy
approximately
\begin{equation}
  t_A-t_B \;\approx\; (C_A-C_B)+T_{\mathrm{ag}}(4N)-T_{\mathrm{ar}}(4P),
  \label{eq:crossover}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm{ag}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{ar}}$ are the times of an all-gather and an
all-reduce at the stated array sizes. The positive gather term is A's additional gather;
the common score gather cancels. It costs only microseconds in these experiments, so
the practical comparison is $C_A-C_B > T_{\mathrm{ar}}(4P)$. Ideal splitting would
make $C_B=C_A/D$, but small computations need not scale that well.

\paragraph{Why perturbation type matters.} Full-rank reconstruction generates or reads
$NP$ noise values before summing them. Low-rank reconstruction can combine the factors
with one matrix multiplication per weight matrix:
\[
  \textstyle\sum_i w_i U_iV_i^\top
  =[w_1U_1,\dots,w_NU_N]\,[V_1,\dots,V_N]^\top .
\]
This avoids a full noise array for every candidate. The resulting update is dense in
storage under either placement; with mirrored sampling its matrix rank is at most
$\min(m,n,Nr/2)$. Splitting therefore adds the same model-sized communication for
low-rank noise while saving less reconstruction work. The communication cost also
grows with model size and depends on the connection between devices.

Eq.~\ref{eq:crossover} is an approximate accounting model: separately timed components
can have different costs when combined by the compiler. We measure that difference in
Section~\ref{subsec:block}. The equation was developed after the single-host sweeps;
its original host-boundary prediction and subsequent correction are reported separately
in Section~\ref{subsec:boundary}. Other placements and the possible overlap of
computation with communication are discussed in Appendix~\ref{sec:timemodel}.
