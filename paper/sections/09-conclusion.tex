\section{Conclusion}

``ES only communicates scalars'' is true for one placement of the update and a poor
guide to which placement is faster. The choice follows Eq.~\ref{eq:crossover}: all-reduce
the partial sums when the share of the sum that saves takes longer than a model-sized
all-reduce, and repeat the sum on every device otherwise. On one A100 node and one v5e
host that means all-reduce for full-rank noise and replicating for low-rank noise at large
width, the same on a real 0.5B model; across two hosts on a slow link the full-rank arm
switches too, as the equation predicts. Low-rank noise saves more on the TPU than on the
GPU. At equal population its updates align with the exact gradient about as well as
full-rank ones, and on Countdown it reaches the same accuracy at 1.44$\times$ the update
rate, while full rank gets there in fewer samples.

\paragraph{What to do differently.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Time your sum and your model-sized all-reduce, and use Eq.~\ref{eq:crossover}. On
    a single host with a fast interconnect, all-reduce for full-rank noise and replicate
    for low-rank noise at large width, where replicating is ahead by 4--15\% on the A100
    and 17--46\% on the v5e. Across a slow host boundary, replicate.
  \item Keep fitness in float32: in bfloat16 a population's losses collapse to a handful
    of ties, and ranks of ties are noise.
  \item Do not force full-precision matrix multiplies on a low-rank run; it costs
    1.6--1.9$\times$ there and almost nothing for full-rank noise.
  \item Above about $10^4$ members, budget for the ranking sort, which every device
    repeats.
\end{itemize}
