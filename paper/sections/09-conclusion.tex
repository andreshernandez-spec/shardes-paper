\section{Conclusion}

Exchanging only scalar scores makes every device repeat the whole update reconstruction.
Splitting that reconstruction is faster when the computation it saves outweighs the
communication of a model-sized update. With eight devices in one host, splitting is faster for
full-rank perturbations and replication for low-rank perturbations at width 2048, and the
faster placement is the same on Qwen2.5-0.5B on the TPU. Across a slow connection between two
A100 hosts, replication is faster for both perturbation types at width 2048. In the block's
single-device cost grid (Figure~\ref{fig:f4}), rank-1 generations take
\RankOneOverDenseGPU{} times as long as dense ones on the A100 and \RankOneOverDenseTPU{}
times as long on the v5e, as geometric means over matched configurations.

A placement is best chosen by measuring the reconstruction and the added communication inside
the program that will run them. Eq.~\ref{eq:crossover} identifies the faster placement in all
\ModelResolved{} resolved block configurations on eight devices within one host, and in all
\HostExpectedMatches{} configurations under both two-host layouts, but its estimates of the
time difference remain approximate: on sixteen devices it overestimates the penalty for
splitting by up to \HostExpectedOverSixteen\,ms.
