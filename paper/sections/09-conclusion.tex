\section{Conclusion}

Exchanging only scalar scores makes every device repeat the whole update reconstruction.
Splitting that reconstruction is faster when the computation it saves outweighs the
communication of a model-sized update. With eight devices in one host, splitting is faster for
full-rank perturbations and replication for low-rank perturbations at width 2048, and the
faster placement is the same on Qwen2.5-0.5B on the TPU. Across a slow connection between two
A100 hosts, replication is faster for both perturbation types at width 2048. Low-rank
perturbations also shorten the generation itself: on the block, a rank-1 generation takes
\RankOneOverDenseGPU{} times as long as a dense one on the A100 and \RankOneOverDenseTPU{}
times as long on the v5e.

A placement is best chosen by measuring the reconstruction and the added communication inside
the program that will run them. Eq.~\ref{eq:crossover} gives the faster placement in all
\ModelResolved{} resolved single-host configurations and in all \HostExpectedMatches{}
two-host configurations, but it is an accounting model, not an exact rule: on sixteen devices
it overestimates the penalty for splitting by up to \HostExpectedOverSixteen\,ms.
