\section{Conclusion}

``ES only communicates scalars'' is true for one placement of the update and a poor
guide to which placement is faster. Splitting the sum helps when the computation it
saves takes longer than the model-sized all-reduce it adds. On one A100 node and one v5e
host that means all-reduce for full-rank noise and replicating for low-rank noise at
large width, also on a real 0.5B model. Low-rank noise saves more on the TPU than on the
GPU, aligns with the gradient-descent direction about as well at equal population, and
on Countdown, updating all parameters, ends at similar accuracy, behind full rank at the
first checkpoint.

\paragraph{What to do differently.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Time your sum and your model-sized all-reduce, both inside the program, and
    compare them with Eq.~\ref{eq:crossover}. On a single host with a fast interconnect
    we found all-reduce faster for full-rank noise, and replication faster for low-rank
    noise at large width, where the all-reduce took 4--15\% longer on the A100 and
    17--46\% longer on the v5e. Across a slow host boundary the all-reduce kept its lead only
    where the model-sized buffer was small: full-rank noise at width 512 (6\,MiB).
  \item Keep fitness in float32: in bfloat16 a population's losses collapse to a handful
    of ties, and ranks of ties are noise.
  \item Forcing full-precision matrix multiplies cost the low-rank arms 1.6--1.9$\times$
    in time and the full-rank arms at most 8\%; whether it buys any accuracy we did not
    measure, so check before paying for it.
  \item Above about $10^4$ members, budget for the ranking sort, which every device
    repeats.
\end{itemize}
