\begin{abstract}
Evolution strategies (ES) lend themselves well to distributed workloads because most of
the work is independent. Forming the parameter update requires combining results from all devices.
We compare the two main ways to do this. After the devices share their scalar fitness
scores, either every device rebuilds the same full update, repeating the computation
(replication), or each device builds only its share and the devices exchange model-sized
partial updates (splitting). The faster choice depends on
whether the computation saved outweighs the communication added. Using one JAX implementation,
we measure this tradeoff for full-rank and low-rank perturbations on A100 GPUs and TPU v5e
chips. On a transformer-block benchmark with eight devices in one host, splitting gives
\FullRankSpeedup$\times$ speedups for full-rank variants without mirrored sampling; for rank-1
perturbations at matrix width 2048, it takes \LowRankSlowdown\% longer than replication.
A timing model using separately measured costs identifies the faster placement in all
\ModelResolved{} block configurations with a clear timing difference. The placement preference
carries over to Qwen2.5-0.5B on the TPU. Across a slow connection between two A100 hosts,
replication is faster for full-rank and rank-1 perturbations at width 2048, reversing the
full-rank preference. Code, configurations, and results are released.
\end{abstract}
