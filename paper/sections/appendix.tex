\section{Scaling grids}
\label{sec:grids}

Figures~\ref{fig:m1-a100}--\ref{fig:m2-tpu} are the full strong- and weak-scaling grids,
every arm under both placements. Weak scaling is shown as parallel efficiency, each series
against its own smallest measured device count; the lower row of the weak-scaling figures
is peak memory per device. At $d{=}2048$ and 32 members per device the rank-1 arms keep
0.86--0.93 efficiency at eight devices on the A100 and 0.71--0.91 on the v5e. What
degrades is a placement: under replication the dense and seed arms fall to 0.39 and 0.50
on the A100 (0.18 and 0.54 on the v5e), because they have the most work to repeat, and
under all-reduce the same arms hold 0.79--1.00. The v5e grid has efficiencies above one,
all at $d{=}512$ (rank 1 at $N{=}1024$ reaches 1.22 at $D{=}2$): there the single chip is
memory-bound, and splitting the population relieves it. The dense arm with all-reduce has
no one-device point at $d{=}2048$, $N{=}256$ on the v5e (out of memory), so its efficiency
is normalized at $D{=}2$.

\section{The time model, cell by cell}
\label{sec:timemodel}

Eq.~\ref{eq:crossover} with the isolated collective of Table~\ref{tab:tb8} and a
contraction $C$ timed on its own (\texttt{contraction\_isolation.py}) leaves a residual in
eight of the eleven cells where replication wins: B is slower than its local sum plus
the isolated all-reduce account for. Two measured effects explain most of it, and both are
largest where the sum is smallest. First, B's local sum does not shrink to $C/D$: measured
as $C/(D\,C_{\mathrm{local}})$ it reaches 0.60--1.01 for dense and seed noise, where $C$
is 3.6--61\,ms and the per-device fixed cost amortizes, and only 0.18--0.46 for rank 1,
where $C$ is 0.15--1.3\,ms. Second, the same 96\,MiB all-reduce costs
1.75--1.80$\times$ its isolated step time inside the low-rank programs and
1.15--1.41$\times$ inside the dense ones, the lean program having less compute to hide
it behind.

With both measured terms substituted, the four low-rank $d{=}2048$ residuals fall from
0.98--1.17\,ms to 0.012--0.224\,ms, and across the A100 grid the median absolute
residual falls from 1.13 to 0.63\,ms, with the sign right at 18 of 20 cells. Bootstrapping
each cell's difference of medians, nine of the twenty residuals exceed twice their noise;
that noise is measured within one process after one compilation, so nine is an upper
bound on the cells genuinely unexplained. Two stand out, both dense at $d{=}512$, where
replication is 1.5 and 5.4\,ms slower than the model says. Rerun on a second node of the
same type, the $N{=}256$ residual reproduces (1.6\,ms) and the $N{=}1024$ one becomes
$-0.6$\,ms, so the latter was the host. For the one that survives, the two obvious
explanations were measured and rejected: holding a population-sized buffer (up to
3\,GiB per device) during the isolated measurement moves it by under 0.31\%, and feeding
the contraction from the evaluation instead of handing it the weights changes a
one-device run by about 1\,ms where the effect would need 7. On the v5e the same
decomposition finds both effects at the same sizes ($C/(D\,C_{\mathrm{local}})$ of
0.95--1.00 for dense and seed noise against 0.16--0.62 for rank 1; in-program
all-reduce at 1.18--1.60$\times$ its isolated time) and the sign right at 15 of 16 cells.

\section{The real model's memory}
\label{sec:memory}

In Figure~\ref{fig:f9} rank 1 does not fit a single 16\,GB chip even at $N{=}32$, while
the full-rank arm runs everywhere, which suggests that carrying factors costs memory. It
does not: ranks 1, 4 and 16 run out of memory at identical shapes, with the compiler's
reported temporaries agreeing to 0.1\%, where stored factors would scale with $r$. The
boundary is a function of members per device, and the term is activations. The full-rank
arm scans its population one member at a time, while the low-rank arms evaluate each
device's whole population in one batch, which is where their speed comes from (the base
weight is shared under the batch, so members share one multiply). Giving the full-rank
arm the same batching reproduces the low-rank memory curve. The placement ratios of
Figure~\ref{fig:f9} are unaffected, since A and B are the same arm at the same shape.
Table~\ref{tab:tb5} gives the absolute times.

\section{The ranking step, priced}
\label{sec:barrier}

Measured in isolation over 84 cells on the v5e, going from one device to eight adds
9--10\,$\mu$s to the ranking step at populations up to a thousand, and 26\,$\mu$s at
$N{=}2^{18}$, where the 1\,MiB gather is bandwidth-bound; the 16\,$\mu$s between the two
is what Table~\ref{tab:tb8} gives for that gather on its own. The sort grows faster: 12.1\,ms
at $N{=}2^{18}$, the same within 0.3\% on one device and eight, about 46\,ns per member,
because every chip sorts the same replicated array. At populations up to a thousand the
whole step is 60\,$\mu$s or less against generations of milliseconds; at four thousand it
is 0.2\,ms, and at the tens of thousands EGGROLL targets it approaches a millisecond. In
full generations with shaping on and off at the largest cells measured, the difference is
41\,$\mu$s ($d{=}512$, $N{=}1024$) and 149\,$\mu$s ($d{=}2048$, $N{=}256$), inside the
repeat spread.

Figure~\ref{fig:f5} is the synthetic study behind Section~\ref{sec:quality}. Its x-axis
is the one the frozen predictions used: $d_{\mathrm{samp}}$ is the number of independent
random numbers in one perturbation, the parameter count for full-rank noise and $r(m+n)$
per $m\times n$ matrix at rank $r$ (other parameters count fully). The low-rank curves sit
lower against this axis, and their smaller $d_{\mathrm{samp}}$ moves an equal-$N$
comparison to the right, which is why at equal population the arms nearly coincide. The
null result for orthogonal and quasi-random sampling holds for this high-dimensional,
small-population regime; it is not a claim about coupled sampling in general.

Table~\ref{tab:tb3} collects the timing ablations; the main text uses the rank and
precision rows. Two differences from the reference code are properties of the released
implementations, not of the algorithms. The reference rank-1 path does not compile on a
16\,GB TPU chip at $d{=}512$, $N{=}16384$: the compiler allocates 24.1\,GiB of temporaries,
reproducible without a TPU by the compile-only probe released with the experiments. The
library works around the same pathology by padding rank-1 factors with one zero column on
the TPU so the compiler keeps a fused multiply, and runs that cell in 245\,ms; the GPU
compiler does not need the pad and it is off there. The reference also declines embedding
tables, which the library perturbs through the same layer seam as matrices.

\section{Reproduction}
\label{sec:repro}

Every figure and table regenerates from a committed script against a committed results
directory, and each results directory's README records the session that produced it
(platform, commit, environment, cost, incidents). Figure~\ref{fig:f2} comes from
\texttt{plot\_paper.py}; Figures~\ref{fig:f4}, \ref{fig:f5}, \ref{fig:f7}, \ref{fig:f7c}
and~\ref{fig:f9} from \texttt{plot\_cost.py}, \texttt{phase0/plot.py},
\texttt{plot\_e13.py}, \texttt{plot\_e19.py} and \texttt{plot\_e17.py}; the scaling grids
from \texttt{plot.py}. Table~\ref{tab:tb1} comes from \texttt{tb1.py},
Table~\ref{tab:tb8} from \texttt{tb8.py}, Table~\ref{tab:tb7} from
\texttt{multihost/tb7\_e18.py}, Table~\ref{tab:tb3} from \texttt{tb3.py},
Table~\ref{tab:tb6} from \texttt{tb6\_e16.py} and Table~\ref{tab:tb5} from
\texttt{tb5\_e17.py}; each accepts \texttt{--latex} and writes the file this document
inputs. The overlap bound of Section~\ref{sec:placement} is \texttt{overlap\_bound.py},
and the byte counts come from the compiled programs (\texttt{comms.py},
\texttt{tb2.py}). The library's tests run on CPU with eight simulated devices, so the
device-count invariance test needs no accelerator. The library is at \libraryurl{}; the
experiments and this manuscript are at \paperurl{}, where \texttt{make -C paper} rebuilds
every table and the PDF, and continuous integration fails if a regenerated table changes
by a byte or a commit a record cites can no longer be fetched.
