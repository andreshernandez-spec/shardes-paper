\section{Scaling grids}
\label{sec:grids}

Figures~\ref{fig:m1-a100}--\ref{fig:m2-tpu} are the full strong- and weak-scaling grids,
every arm under both placements. Both show parallel efficiency, each series against its
own smallest measured device count, so the ideal is 1 for every series; the strong-scaling
figures add time per generation, and the weak-scaling figures peak memory per device. At $d{=}2048$ and 32 members per device the rank-1 arms keep
0.86--0.93 efficiency at eight devices on the A100 and 0.71--0.91 on the v5e. What
degrades is a placement: under replication the dense and seed arms fall to 0.39 and 0.50
on the A100 (0.18 and 0.54 on the v5e), because they have the most work to repeat, and
under all-reduce the same arms hold 0.79--1.00. The v5e grid has efficiencies above one,
all at $d{=}512$ (rank 1 at $N{=}1024$ reaches 1.22 at $D{=}2$): there the single chip is
memory-bound, and splitting the population relieves it. The dense arm with all-reduce has
no one-device point at $d{=}2048$, $N{=}256$ on the v5e (out of memory), so its efficiency
is normalized at $D{=}2$.

Eq.~\ref{eq:crossover} with ideal splitting ($C_B = C_A/D$), the isolated collective of
Table~\ref{tab:tb8} and $C_A$ timed on its own (\texttt{contraction\_isolation.py})
leaves a residual in eight of the eleven configurations where replication wins: B is
slower than its local sum plus the isolated all-reduce account for. Two measured effects
explain most of it, and both are largest where the sum is smallest
(\texttt{timemodel.py} prints every range here). First, B's local sum does not shrink to
$C_A/D$: on the A100, $C_A/(D\,C_B)$ is 0.60--1.01 for full-rank noise, where $C_A$ is
3.6--61\,ms and the per-device fixed cost amortizes, and only 0.18--0.46 for rank 1,
where $C_A$ is 0.15--1.3\,ms. Second, at width 2048 the same 96\,MiB all-reduce costs
1.75--1.80$\times$ its isolated step time inside the low-rank programs and
1.15--1.41$\times$ inside the full-rank ones, the lean program having less compute to
hide it behind.

With both measured terms substituted, the four low-rank $d{=}2048$ residuals fall from
0.98--1.17\,ms to 0.012--0.224\,ms, and across the A100 grid the median absolute
residual falls from 1.13 to 0.63\,ms, with the sign right at 18 of 20 configurations. Bootstrapping
each configuration's difference of medians, nine of the twenty residuals exceed twice
their noise; that noise is measured within one process after one compilation, so nine is
an upper bound on the configurations genuinely unexplained. Two stand out, both dense at $d{=}512$, where
replication is 1.5 and 5.4\,ms slower than the model says. Rerun on a second node of the
same type, the $N{=}256$ residual reproduces (1.6\,ms) and the $N{=}1024$ one becomes
$-0.6$\,ms, so the latter was the host. For the one that survives, the two obvious
explanations were measured and rejected: holding a population-sized buffer (up to
3\,GiB per device) during the isolated measurement moves it by under 0.31\%, and feeding
the sum from the evaluation instead of handing it the weights changes a
one-device run by about 1\,ms where the effect would need 7. On the v5e the same
decomposition finds both effects at the same sizes ($C_A/(D\,C_B)$ of 0.97--1.07 for
full-rank noise against 0.16--0.62 for rank 1; the in-program all-reduce at
1.04--1.60$\times$ its isolated time for rank 1) and the sign right at 15 of 16
configurations.

\section{The real model's memory}
\label{sec:memory}

In Figure~\ref{fig:f9} rank 1 does not fit a single 16\,GB chip even at $N{=}32$, while
the full-rank arm runs everywhere, which suggests that carrying factors costs memory. It
does not: ranks 1, 4 and 16 run out of memory at identical shapes, with the compiler's
reported temporaries agreeing to 0.1\%, where stored factors would scale with $r$. The
boundary is a function of members per device, and the term is activations. The full-rank
arm scans its population one member at a time, while the low-rank arms evaluate each
device's whole population in one batch, which is where their speed comes from (the base
weight is shared under the batch, so members share one multiply). Giving the full-rank
arm the same batching reproduces the low-rank memory curve. The placement ratios of
Figure~\ref{fig:f9} are unaffected, since A and B are the same arm at the same shape.
Table~\ref{tab:tb5} gives the absolute times.

Measured in isolation over 84 configurations on the v5e, going from one device to eight adds
9--10\,$\mu$s to the ranking step at populations up to a thousand, and 26\,$\mu$s at
$N{=}2^{18}$, where the 1\,MiB gather is bandwidth-bound; the 16\,$\mu$s between the two
is what Table~\ref{tab:tb8} gives for that gather on its own. The sort grows faster: 12.1\,ms
at $N{=}2^{18}$, the same within 0.3\% on one device and eight, about 46\,ns per member,
because every chip sorts the same replicated array. At populations up to a thousand the
whole step is 60\,$\mu$s or less against generations of milliseconds; at four thousand it
is 0.2\,ms, and at the tens of thousands EGGROLL targets it approaches a millisecond. In
full generations with shaping on and off at the largest configurations measured, the difference is
41\,$\mu$s ($d{=}512$, $N{=}1024$) and 149\,$\mu$s ($d{=}2048$, $N{=}256$), inside the
repeat spread.

\paragraph{The Gaussian-projection approximation.} Let $z_i$ be member $i$'s
perturbation projected onto the unit descent direction, $\langle\epsilon_i, -\nabla
L\rangle/\lVert\nabla L\rVert$. It has unit variance for full-rank and for low-rank
noise, since $AB^\top/\sqrt r$ has identity covariance. If the loss is locally linear,
ranking the members by loss ranks them by $z_i$, and if $z_i$ is Gaussian each
$\epsilon_i$ enters Eq.~\ref{eq:update} with a coefficient close to $\Phi(z_i)-\tfrac12$
for large $N$. The update's component along the
descent direction is then about $N\,\mathbb{E}[(\Phi(z)-\tfrac12)\,z] = N/(2\sqrt\pi)$
and, for $N\ll P$, its norm is about $\sqrt{NP\,\mathbb{E}[(\Phi(z)-\tfrac12)^2]} =
\sqrt{NP/12}$, so the cosine is $\sqrt{3/\pi}\,\sqrt{N/P}$. A mirrored pair contributes
one direction with twice the weight, and $N/2$ of them give $1/\sqrt2$ of that.

\paragraph{When the projection is Gaussian.} For full-rank Gaussian noise $z_i$ is
exactly Gaussian. For rank-$r$ noise on a matrix with gradient $G$, the projection is a
sum over the $r$ factor pairs of $a^\top G b$, summed again over matrices. If $G$ has
rank one, $G = uv^\top$, each term is $(a^\top u)(v^\top b)$, a product of two Gaussians,
however many entries of $G$ are nonzero. The projection approaches a Gaussian as the
gradient spreads over many singular directions and many matrices, and as $r$ grows. We
did not measure the gradient's spectrum on Qwen, so for the real model the approximation
is a scale estimate, not a derivation.

Figure~\ref{fig:f5} is the synthetic study behind Section~\ref{sec:quality}, one slice
of 456 configurations of 30 replicates each. Against $N/P$ the mirrored arms of every
rank coincide, the largest over the smallest at equal $N$ being 1.01--1.04, and every
arm is within 0.91--1.02 of the approximation; its log-log slopes are 0.494--0.508. Unpaired sampling lies 1.34--1.53$\times$ above mirrored, about $\sqrt2$.
Orthogonal (Hadamard) and scrambled Sobol sampling, not drawn, are within 0.97--1.04 of
plain mirroring. That null result holds for this high-dimensional, small-population
regime; it is not a claim about coupled sampling in general.

The predictions frozen before the real-model runs used $N/d_{\mathrm{samp}}$ instead,
where $d_{\mathrm{samp}}$ is the number of independent random numbers in one
perturbation: the parameter count for full-rank noise and $r(m+n)$ per $m\times n$
matrix at rank $r$ (other parameters count fully). Each arm's own fit against that axis
was the prediction, and Table~\ref{tab:tb6-audit} keeps it next to the synthetic
reference. On the block, where every matrix is $512\times512$, $P/d_{\mathrm{samp}}$ is
256 at rank 1 and 64 at rank 4. On Qwen2.5 the matrices are larger and not square, and it
is 638 and 171 at 0.5B and 1063 and 287 at 1.5B, so the same $N$ sits further right on
the $N/d_{\mathrm{samp}}$ axis than on the block and the fit reads a higher cosine. At a
slope of one half that overstates rank 1 by $\sqrt{638/256}\approx1.6$ and rank 4 by
$\sqrt{171/64}\approx1.6$ at 0.5B, against measured shortfalls of 1.9--2.0 and 1.5--1.8.
The same fits predicted full rank within 9\%, since $P/d_{\mathrm{samp}}$ is 1 for it on
every model. For rank 1 at 1.5B the prediction was multiplied by a correction of 0.53
measured at 0.5B and fixed in advance; the observations were still 23--26\% below it.

\section{Systems ablations and reference boundaries}
\label{sec:systems-ablations}
\label{sec:refimpl}

Table~\ref{tab:tb3} collects the timing ablations; the main text uses the rank and
precision rows. Two differences from the reference code are properties of the released
implementations, not of the algorithms. The reference rank-1 path does not compile on a
16\,GB TPU chip at $d{=}512$, $N{=}16384$: the compiler allocates 24.1\,GiB of temporaries,
reproducible without a TPU by the compile-only probe released with the experiments. The
library works around the same pathology by padding rank-1 factors with one zero column on
the TPU so the compiler keeps a fused multiply, and runs that configuration in 245\,ms; the GPU
compiler does not need the pad and it is off there. The reference also declines embedding
tables, which the library perturbs through the same layer seam as matrices.
